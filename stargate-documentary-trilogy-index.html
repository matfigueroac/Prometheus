Stargate documentary trilogy index

What is

This note does not replace the three documentaries: it orders them. Its function is to serve as a master map of the Stargate / CIA / remote viewing package within the wiki, separating layers that appear mixed in the documentary itself:

  • verifiable program history
  • insider testimonials
  • strong operational claims
  • cosmological/paranormal speculation
  • contemporary audiovisual narrative

central thesis of the trilogy

The trilogy attempts to move the conversation from esto fue puro folklore de psychic spies towards a more ambitious thesis:

1. Yes, there was a serious institutional ecosystem around remote viewing

2. some historical protocols and actors were real and verifiable

3. the program produced enough signal not to rule it out as trivial fraud

4. The public closure of the program does not equate to the nonexistence of the phenomenon

5. Behind Stargate There Might Be a Bigger Problem About Consciousness, Nonlocal Perception, and Anomalies

How to read the trilogy correctly

Layer 1 — verifiable history

The most solid thing about the package is that there was:

  • SRI
  • DIA / CIA / Army / Fort Meade
  • declassified documents
  • real historical names
  • sessions, memos and tasking within the Stargate ecosystem

Layer 2 — testimonial defense of the program

Enter McMoneagle, Edwin May, Hal Puthoff, Ingo Swann and others. The strength is in the insider knowledge and the historical context, but it is still testimonial material.

Layer 3 — extraordinary claims

Here are the most fragile jumps:

  • very high hit rates without visible full audit
  • spectacular operations difficult to verify
  • Uri Geller
  • ancient mars
  • Monroe Institute as a door to expanded states of consciousness

Cover 4 — cultural narrative

The trilogy is also important as a contemporary cultural piece: it organizes decades of Stargate lore for a modern audience and connects psychic spies with UFO/UAP, consciousness, anomalies, and mental exploration.

Narrative line of the three parts

Part 1 — installation of the operational myth

how-the-cia-spent-millions-on-psychic-spies-project-stargate-documentary-1

  • install Cold War + US/USSR psi race
  • features SRI, Ingo Swann, McMoneagle and Ed Dames
  • explains coordinate remote viewing as a technique
  • mixes historical archive, modern training and demonstrations
  • main function: open the Stargate world and populate it with names

Part 2 — insider defense + controversy

how-the-cia-spent-millions-on-psychic-spies-project-stargate-documentary-2

  • shifts focus to McMoneagle and Edwin May
  • attempts to give greater operational and scientific weight to the program
  • introduces metrics, oversight, agencies and claimed results
  • adds the Uri Geller block, which raises the profile but also the controversy
  • main function: argue that the program produced something more serious than simple folklore

Part 3 — Mars, Monroe and maximum expansion

how-the-cia-spent-millions-on-psychic-spies-project-stargate-documentary-3

  • spin on session Mars 1,000,000 BC
  • connects remote viewing with out-of-body states and Monroe Institute
  • expands the field from intelligence to cosmology and extreme anomalies
  • closes with an open position to continue exploring the phenomenon
  • main function: take Stargate to its most mythical and strange edge

Map of key actors

Historical/experimental core

  • Ingo Swann
  • Joseph McMoneagle
  • Hal Puthoff
  • Russell Targ
  • Edwin May

Military/narrative/media core

  • Ed Dames
  • Brett Stewart
  • Nelson Dellis

Controversial/borderline core

  • Uri Geller
  • Robert Monroe

Claims repeated in the trilogy

  • Stargate and its precursors would have produced useful information for real agencies
  • part of the work would have been done with double-blind or relatively controlled protocols
  • some prominent viewers would have results well above chance
  • The closure or public reduction of the program would not imply total failure of the phenomenon
  • remote viewing would touch on a broader issue of non-local awareness

Relatively strong evidence within the material

  • Historical existence of Gondola Wish/Stargate and relationship with SRI, DIA, CIA and Fort Meade
  • presence of verifiable names and real documents
  • narrative continuity between memo, laboratory, tasking, training and subsequent culture
  • high value to map the Stargate ecosystem as an institutional and cultural phenomenon

Weak or highly debatable evidence

  • success figures presented in testimonial format without complete audit reproducible on screen
  • extraordinary operational anecdotes difficult to independently confirm
  • extrapolation from limited experiments to strong ontological claims
  • Uri Geller block as a focus of ambiguity and controversy
  • Mars 1,000,000 BC as strong cultural document/lore but very weak empirical evidence of ancient Martian civilization
