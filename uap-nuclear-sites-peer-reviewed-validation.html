Palomar sky plates, nuclear tests and UAP reports

Explore a historical study of Palomar sky plates, nuclear-test dates and UAP reports. Compare the observed rates, the authors’ interpretation and the limits of the data.

Summary

Stephen Bruehl and Beatriz Villarroel compared dates of short-lived, star-like marks on photographic plates with atmospheric nuclear tests and historical UAP reports. Their study covers 2,718 days between November 1949 and April 1957.

Added primary sources

Published paper in Scientific Reports · Earlier Research Square preprint · Open full text and tables

What the papers say

The authors counted days on which at least one transient appeared. They compared days within one day of a nuclear test with days outside that window.

Inside the test window
15.6%
Outside the window
10.8%

The difference is 4.8 percentage points. In relative terms, a transient was about 45% more likely inside the window. A separate analysis estimated an 8.5% increase in the count of transients for each additional UAP report on a date. Read the methods and results.

Published vs. preprint

  • The core of the finding is the same in both documents
  • The preprint more strongly emphasizes the association between total transients and total UAP reports by date
  • the published version summarizes that point more conservatively and specifically highlights the subset of days with at least one transient identified (p = 0.015)
  • operational reading: the published paper maintains the hypothesis, but with more cautious framing than the preprint

Comparative critical evaluation

  • remains a historical correlational study, not direct evidence of ships, technologies or causality
  • The inference depends on the quality of three layers of data: transient identification, nuclear test schedule and historical base of UAP reports.
  • there is an important interpretive jump between temporal association and intelligent anomalous phenomenon
  • The strongest part of the material is that there is a peer-reviewed quantitative analysis of a hypothesis normally treated only as UAP folklore.
  • the least solid is the strong conclusion: the data suggest an interesting statistical signal, but do not by themselves validate an extraordinary explanation

Useful reading for X Files

Three records enter the analysis: plate detections, test dates and sighting reports. Comparing their dates can reveal an association. Identifying what produced an individual mark on a plate requires independent evidence.
