A source can answer one question and leave another open. A session transcript records what someone said. An orbital image lets you inspect the shape and lighting of a landform.

Purpose

Start with a specific claim. Find the earliest source available, describe what you can observe and record what would change your interpretation.

Dossier fields

Use these fields when creating or upgrading notes:

  • Claim / object of study: what is being examined, not what must be believed.
  • What the source actually says: concrete statements, dates, quotes, images, transcripts or documents.
  • Evidence type: primary document, peer-reviewed paper, official statement, transcript, media analysis, folklore, testimony, speculation.
  • Source quality: primary, secondary, tertiary, aggregator, unknown, or mixed.
  • Critical reading: mundane explanations, methodological weaknesses, bias, uncertainty and counter-evidence.
  • Why it matters: how it connects to UAP, intelligence archives, Mars, ancient civilizations, liminal media or other X Files lanes.
  • Open questions: what needs verification, comparison or future research.
  • Related notes: other dossiers that offer a contrasting example or additional evidence.

Evidence ladder

  1. Primary documents, official datasets, original images, transcripts, papers.
  2. Reputable secondary analysis with citations.
  3. Interviews, podcasts and long-form expert claims.
  4. Aggregators and watchlists.
  5. Speculation, symbolic interpretation, folklore or internet culture.

Publishing rule

The public dossiers are drawn from the Prometheus research notebook. Check the source section for provenance and use the original record when quoting or citing a claim.

Worked example: the face on Mars

Here is how the archive’s fields apply to one photograph.

Summary

A low-resolution Viking image made a mesa in Cydonia look like a face. Later observations let us compare that impression with the terrain.

Claim / object of study

The question is whether the apparent features survive a change in lighting and resolution.

What the source actually says

The comparison puts the Viking view beside two versions of a later Mars Global Surveyor observation. Inspect the image and its credit.

Evidence type

The orbital images record the surface. The impression of a face is a reading of those images.

Source quality

Identify the spacecraft, observation date and image processing. Two versions of one observation share the same underlying data.

Critical reading

Compare the ridge and its shadow across the panels. Which apparent features persist when the illumination changes?

Why it matters within X Files

The case offers a way to examine other photographs whose subjects are difficult to recognize.

Open questions

Which surface details can each camera resolve? What additional viewing angle would help?
