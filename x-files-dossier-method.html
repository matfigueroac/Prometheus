X Files Dossier Method

This note defines the editorial contract for the public X Files wiki app.

Purpose

Every public dossier should help a reader separate signal from mythology without deleting the weirdness that makes the material worth tracking.

Dossier fields

Use these fields when creating or upgrading notes:

  • Claim / object of study: what is being examined, not what must be believed.
  • What the source actually says: concrete statements, dates, quotes, images, transcripts or documents.
  • Evidence type: primary document, peer-reviewed paper, official statement, transcript, media analysis, folklore, testimony, speculation.
  • Source quality: primary, secondary, tertiary, aggregator, unknown, or mixed.
  • Critical reading: mundane explanations, methodological weaknesses, bias, uncertainty and counter-evidence.
  • Why it matters: how it connects to UAP, intelligence archives, Mars, ancient civilizations, liminal media or other X Files lanes.
  • Open questions: what needs verification, comparison or future research.
  • Related notes: internal links that let the public app build a graph.

Evidence ladder

1. Primary documents, official datasets, original images, transcripts, papers.

2. Reputable secondary analysis with citations.

3. Interviews, podcasts and long-form expert claims.

4. Aggregators and watchlists.

5. Speculation, symbolic interpretation, folklore or internet culture.

Publishing rule

The public site must be generated from Obsidian source notes. Do not manually edit generated HTML as the source of truth.

What the source actually says
