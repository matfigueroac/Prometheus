Lake Van, Urartu and the Old Holy Land Thesis

Baseline

Lake Van is the largest lake in Turkey, located in Eastern Anatolia / the Armenian Highlands. It is a saline soda lake in a volcanic region. The broader area was central to the Iron Age kingdom of Urartu, whose territory extended around Lake Van and neighboring highlands.

Known/mentioned sites in the LaCroix lane include:

  • Ayanis / Rusahinili Eidurukai,
  • Kef Kalesi / Adilcevaz area,
  • Çavuştepe,
  • Meher Gate,
  • underwater ruins in Lake Van,
  • mountain temples and megalithic ruins around Van.

LaCroix's thesis

LaCroix frames the Lake Van region as a possible “old Holy Land”: a source zone for sacred architectural and symbolic systems that later appear across Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, Christianity, South America and mystery traditions.

Transcript anchors:

  • 2:20:47 — “Old Holy Land”.
  • 2:23:00 — basalt relief and sacred teachings.
  • 2:24:12 — sun cross / Vatican / pine cone connection.
  • 2:29:25 — esoteric knowledge and comparative motifs.

His public site also emphasizes Van-region tours involving Ayanis, underwater ruins, Kef Kalesi, Çavuştepe and Meher Gate, and presents a larger comparative-symbol project around T-forms, step-pyramid geometry and inverted step-pyramid motifs.

Conventional control layer

The conventional archaeological baseline identifies many Lake Van sites as Urartian or Iron Age. That does not automatically disprove older reuse, but it means any older-origin claim must demonstrate:

  • pre-Urartian construction phases,
  • different stonework under later Urartian layers,
  • independent dating of foundations or quarrying,
  • stratigraphic separation,
  • inscriptions or material culture inconsistent with later attribution.

Key hypothesis: reuse layer vs origin layer

The useful research model is:

1. Origin layer — earliest sacred or architectural construction.

2. Reuse layer — later culture occupies, rebuilds, renames or militarizes the site.

3. Interpretation layer — modern archaeology names the site by the best-documented later layer.

4. X Files question — whether physical evidence preserves a deeper layer.

Symbol program to track

| Motif | Claimed meaning / role | Verification need |

|---|---|---|

| T-form / T-pillar | Human/temple/axis symbolism | Artifact/site photographs and dates |

Critical reading

What is useful:

  • Lake Van is a real, historically rich region with major Urartian archaeology.
  • The area deserves a detailed site-by-site dossier.
  • LaCroix’s fieldwork creates leads worth tracking.
  • The region may connect Anatolian, Mesopotamian and highland cultural flows.

What is not yet established:

  • that Lake Van is the original “Holy Land” of later world religions,
  • that global symbol recurrence proves contact,
  • that Urartian sites are actually much older without direct evidence,
  • that vitrified basalt indicates plasma/CME catastrophe.

Research tasks

  • Build separate pages for Ayanis, Kef Kalesi/Adilcevaz, Çavuştepe, Meher Gate and Lake Van underwater ruins.
  • Verify site names and spellings from primary/academic sources.
  • Collect artifact images and distinguish Urartian iconography from alternative interpretation.
  • Find geological discussion of basalt, volcanism and any vitrification claims.
